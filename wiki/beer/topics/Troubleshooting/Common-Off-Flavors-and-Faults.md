## Summary

Common beer off-flavors, their usual causes, and what to check next time.

## Details

- **Diacetyl (butter/butterscotch)** — often incomplete fermentation or too-early cold crash; try a diacetyl rest (raise temp a few degrees near the end of fermentation) before packaging.
- **Acetaldehyde (green apple)** — usually just means the beer needs more conditioning time.
- **Astringency (puckering, tannic)** — oversparging, sparge water too hot, or crushed grain steeped too long/too hot.
- **Autolysis (rubbery, meaty)** — beer left too long on a large yeast cake at warm temperature; rack off the yeast sooner next time.
- **Infection (sour when it shouldn't be, ropy, off-odors)** — almost always a sanitation gap; review contact points (siphon tubing, bottling wand, fermenter lid).
- **Oxidation (wet cardboard, sherry-like)** — excess splashing/aeration after fermentation, or too much headspace/time in the bottle/keg.
