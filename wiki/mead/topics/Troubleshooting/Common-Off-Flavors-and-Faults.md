## Summary

Common mead off-flavors, their usual causes, and what to check next time.

## Details

- **Hot/harsh alcohol** — usually just needs more bulk aging; can also mean fermentation temperature was too high.
- **Rotten egg (sulfur) / yeast bite** — classic sign of nutrient-starved fermentation; follow a staggered nutrient schedule next time.
- **Cidery/solvent notes** — fermentation too warm, or fusel alcohols from a stressed, high-gravity fermentation.
- **Thin/watery** — common in traditional (varietal-only) meads; a small acid addition or longer aging can help balance.
- **Stuck fermentation** — high starting gravity outrunning the yeast's alcohol tolerance, or nutrient deficiency; check `OG` against the strain's stated tolerance.
- **Honey character faded to nothing** — some yeast strains ferment out varietal character more than others; note the strain used for next time.
