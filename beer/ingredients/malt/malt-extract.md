# Malt Extract
Malt extract is wort that a maltster has mashed, then evaporated. You rehydrate it, add hops and boil. It is unhopped, unlike a [kit can](/beer/ingredients/malt/cans-of-goo).

## Types

| Type | Moisture | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquid malt extract (LME)** | ~20% | Syrup. Cheaper, but ages and darkens. Use within a few months |
| **Dry malt extract (DME)** | ~2% | Powder. Longer shelf life, easier to measure, ideal for starters |

## Approximate Yield
Points per pound per gallon (ppg): LME ~36, DME ~44. In metric, DME is about 1.0 kg in 10 L for roughly 1.048.

## Colours
Extracts come as extra light, light, amber, dark and wheat. Light extract with steeped specialty grain gives the most flexibility.

## Using Extract Well
- **Add it off the heat** to prevent scorching, then return to the boil.
- **Late addition.** Adding most of the extract in the last 15 minutes keeps colour pale and improves hop utilisation.
- **Use fresh extract.** Stale extract tastes of tinned fruit or molasses.
- **Beware "extract twang".** A harsh, sweet, slightly metallic character from old, scorched or over-boiled extract.
