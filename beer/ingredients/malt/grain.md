# Grain
Malted grain is the starting point for all grain and partial mash brewing. It must be crushed (milled) and then [mashed](/beer/techniques/mashing).

## Base Malts
Make up most of the grist. They have enough enzymes to convert their own starch, and often more.

| Malt | Colour (SRM) | Flavour | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilsner | 1 - 2 | Clean, grainy, slightly sweet | Lagers, Belgian ales |
| Pale 2-row | 2 - 3 | Neutral, malty | American styles |
| Maris Otter | 3 - 4 | Rich, biscuity, nutty | English ales |
| Vienna | 3 - 5 | Toasty, bready | Amber lagers |
| Munich | 8 - 20 | Bready, melanoidin | Bocks, Oktoberfest |
| Wheat malt | 2 - 3 | Soft, bready | Weissbier, Witbier |

## Specialty Malts
Used in small amounts for colour and flavour. Most have few or no enzymes.

| Malt | Colour (SRM) | Flavour |
|---|---|---|
| Crystal / Caramel | 10 - 120 | Sweetness, caramel, toffee, raisin |
| Biscuit / Victory | 25 - 30 | Dry toast, biscuit |
| Chocolate | 350 | Cocoa, dry roast |
| Roasted barley | 500 | Coffee, acrid. Defines dry stout |
| Black patent | 500+ | Sharp, burnt |

## Adjuncts
Unmalted grain and sugars: flaked oats (silky body), flaked maize and rice (lighter body), wheat, rye, candi sugar and dextrose.

## Practical Notes
- **Crush.** Grain should be cracked, not powdered. Husks should remain mostly intact.
- **Freshness.** Use crushed grain within a few weeks. Whole grain keeps for 6 - 12 months in a cool, dry place.
- **Typical yield.** About 35 - 37 ppg for base malts at 100% efficiency; most brewers achieve 65 - 80%.
- **Steeping.** Specialty malts can be steeped like tea in [extract brewing](/beer/beginners/brewing-process) without mashing. Base malts must be mashed.
