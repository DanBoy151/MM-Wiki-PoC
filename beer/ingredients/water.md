# Water
Beer is about 90% water, so it matters, but new brewers should not worry too much. If tap water tastes good to drink, it will usually make acceptable beer.

## Step 1: Remove Chlorine and Chloramine
Chlorine and chloramine combine with phenols from malt to produce medicinal "band-aid" flavours.

- **Chlorine:** boil, or let water stand overnight.
- **Chloramine:** does not boil off. Use a carbon filter or a Campden tablet (potassium or sodium metabisulphite) - about 1 tablet per 20 gallons / 75 L.

## Key Minerals

| Ion | Typical range (ppm) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Calcium | 50 - 150 | Yeast health, clarity, enzyme activity, lowers mash pH |
| Magnesium | 5 - 30 | Yeast nutrient. High levels taste sour or bitter |
| Sodium | 0 - 150 | Rounds out flavour. High levels taste salty |
| Sulfate | 50 - 350 | Accentuates hop bitterness, dryness |
| Chloride | 50 - 150 | Accentuates malt, fullness, sweetness |
| Bicarbonate | 0 - 250 | Raises mash pH. Needed for dark beers, bad for pale beers |

## Sulfate to Chloride Ratio
A quick guide to balance:

| Ratio (SO4:Cl) | Character |
|---|---|
| Below 0.5 | Very malty |
| 0.5 - 2 | Balanced |
| Above 2 | Bitter, crisp |

## Mash pH
Aim for a mash pH of **5.2 - 5.6** (measured at room temperature). Too high gives harsh tannins, too low gives thin, sour beer. Dark malts naturally lower pH. Pale beers made with high-bicarbonate water often need acid (lactic or phosphoric) or dilution with distilled water.

## Water and Style Traditions

| Region | Water | Traditional style |
|---|---|---|
| Plzen (Czech) | Very soft | [Czech Pale Lager](/beer/styles/czech) |
| Burton-on-Trent (England) | High sulfate | Pale ale, IPA |
| Dublin (Ireland) | High bicarbonate | Dry stout |
| Munich (Germany) | Moderate bicarbonate | Dunkel, Helles |

## Practical Advice
1. Get a water report from your supplier.
2. Start with filtered tap or a 50:50 mix with distilled or reverse osmosis water.
3. Adjust with small amounts of gypsum (sulfate), calcium chloride (chloride) and lactic acid.
4. Change one thing at a time and keep notes.
