# Brett
*Brettanomyces* ("British fungus") is a genus of yeast long associated with English stock ales, Belgian lambic and saison. It ferments slowly, can eat sugars Saccharomyces cannot and produces distinctive flavours.

## Flavour
| Descriptor | Strain tendency |
|---|---|
| Barnyard, horse blanket, leather | *B. bruxellensis* (classic funk) |
| Tropical fruit, pineapple, mango | *B. claussenii*, *B. anomalus* and many "clean" commercial strains |
| Cherry, pie spice | *B. lambicus* |

## Ways to Use It
- **Secondary fermentation.** Add to finished beer to dry it out and develop funk over months.
- **Primary with Saccharomyces.** Fermenting together gives fruitiness with less funk.
- **100% Brett.** Fermenting with Brett alone gives clean tropical flavours. Fast, modern commercial strains are available.
- **Bottle conditioning.** Brett consumes oxygen, so it can improve beer stability.

## Handling Cautions
- **Cross-contamination.** Brett can spread to the rest of your brewing equipment. Use dedicated plastic and tubing, and sanitise aggressively.
- **Over-carbonation.** Brett will keep eating complex sugars, so beer must be fully dry or kept in strong bottles.
- **Patience.** Months, not weeks.
