# Bugs
"Bugs" are the bacteria brewers use deliberately to make sour beer.

## Main Players

| Organism | Produces | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ***Lactobacillus*** | Clean lactic acid | Fast. Thrives at 30 - 45 C (86 - 113 F). Intolerant of high hop levels |
| ***Pediococcus*** | Lactic acid, may produce diacetyl | Slow. Tolerates more hops. Can make beer ropey for a while |
| ***Acetobacter*** | Acetic acid (vinegar) | Needs oxygen. Usually unwanted |

## Souring Methods

| Method | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Kettle souring** | Inoculate unhopped wort with Lactobacillus, hold warm for 12 - 48 hours, then boil and ferment normally. Fast and clean |
| **Mixed fermentation** | Pitch Saccharomyces, Brett and bacteria together. Slow (6 - 24 months) and complex |
| **Sour mash** | Spontaneous Lactobacillus from malt husks. Unpredictable |
| **Blending** | Mix young and aged sour beers, as done for gueuze |

## Handling
- **Cross-contamination.** Treat bacteria like [Brett](/beer/ingredients/yeast/brett): dedicated gear and thorough sanitation.
- **Keep it hop-free for Lactobacillus.** Over about 5 - 10 IBU inhibits growth.
- **Flush with CO2 when kettle souring** to keep oxygen out and prevent acetic acid.
- **Safe pH.** Lower than about 4.5 is safe against most pathogens, and the sour wort should reach 3.2 - 3.6.
