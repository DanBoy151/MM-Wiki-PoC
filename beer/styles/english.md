# English Styles
English ales are malt-forward, balanced and traditionally served at cellar temperature with light carbonation. They are an excellent place to start brewing as they are forgiving.

## Common Styles

| Style | OG | ABV | IBU | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mild | 1.030 - 1.038 | 2.8 - 4.5% | 10 - 25 | Light, malty, low bitterness |
| Ordinary / Best Bitter | 1.032 - 1.048 | 3.2 - 5% | 25 - 40 | Balanced, biscuity, earthy hops |
| Extra Special Bitter (ESB) | 1.048 - 1.060 | 4.6 - 6.2% | 30 - 50 | Richer, stronger bitter |
| English IPA | 1.050 - 1.075 | 5 - 7.5% | 40 - 60 | Dry, hop-forward but restrained |
| Brown Ale | 1.040 - 1.052 | 4.2 - 5.4% | 20 - 30 | Nutty, toffee |
| Porter | 1.040 - 1.052 | 4 - 5.4% | 18 - 35 | Dark, toasty, chocolate |
| Stout (dry, sweet, oatmeal) | 1.036 - 1.060 | 4 - 6% | 25 - 45 | Roasted, coffee |

## Key Ingredients
- **Maris Otter** or other English pale malt. See [Grain](/beer/ingredients/malt/grain).
- **Crystal malts** for toffee and dried fruit.
- **English hops**: East Kent Goldings, Fuggle, Challenger, Target.
- **English ale yeast**: fruity esters, flocculent and good for cask. See [Saccharomyces](/beer/ingredients/yeast/saccharomyces).
- **Water** with moderate sulfate, as in Burton. See [Water](/beer/ingredients/water).

## Technique
- [Single infusion](/beer/techniques/mashing/single-infusion) at 66 - 68 C (151 - 154 F).
- Ferment at 18 - 20 C.
- Low carbonation (1.5 - 2.0 volumes), especially if cask-conditioned. See [Packaging](/beer/techniques/packaging).
