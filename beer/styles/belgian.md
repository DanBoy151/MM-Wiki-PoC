# Belgian Styles
Belgian beers are defined by yeast. Fermentation temperature and strain give fruity, spicy and phenolic notes. Beers tend to be highly attenuated, with moderate bitterness.

## Common Styles

| Style | OG | ABV | IBU | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Witbier | 1.044 - 1.052 | 4.5 - 5.5% | 8 - 20 | Wheat, coriander, orange peel |
| Belgian Blonde | 1.062 - 1.075 | 6 - 7.5% | 15 - 30 | Pale, soft, subtle fruit |
| Saison | 1.048 - 1.065 | 3.5 - 9.5% | 20 - 35 | Dry, peppery, fruity, often warm-fermented |
| Dubbel | 1.062 - 1.075 | 6 - 7.6% | 15 - 25 | Dark fruit, caramel, candi syrup |
| Tripel | 1.075 - 1.085 | 7.5 - 9.5% | 20 - 40 | Pale, strong, spicy, dry |
| Belgian Strong Dark | 1.075 - 1.110 | 8 - 12% | 20 - 35 | Rich, complex, dark fruit |

## Key Ingredients
- **Pilsner malt** as a base for pale styles.
- **Belgian candi sugar or syrup** for body, colour and high gravity without sweetness.
- **Spices** such as coriander and bitter orange in Wit.
- **Low to moderate hopping**, with Saaz, Styrian Goldings or Hallertau.

## Fermentation Tips
- Use [Belgian strains](/beer/ingredients/yeast/saccharomyces). Fermentation often starts at 18 C and is allowed to rise to 24 - 28 C.
- Large pitch of healthy yeast is needed for strong beers. See [Starters](/beer/techniques/fermentation/yeast-management/starters).
- Highly carbonated: 2.5 - 4.0 volumes. Use strong bottles. See [Bottle Conditioning](/beer/techniques/packaging/bottle-conditioning).
