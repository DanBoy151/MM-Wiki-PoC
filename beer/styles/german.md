# German Styles
German brewing emphasises clean fermentation, quality malt and restrained hopping. Traditionally brewed under the *Reinheitsgebot* purity law (water, malt, hops, yeast).

## Common Styles

| Style | OG | ABV | IBU | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Helles | 1.044 - 1.048 | 4.7 - 5.4% | 16 - 22 | Soft, bready, balanced lager |
| German Pils | 1.044 - 1.050 | 4.4 - 5.2% | 22 - 40 | Crisp, bitter, dry |
| Märzen / Oktoberfest | 1.054 - 1.060 | 5.8 - 6.3% | 18 - 24 | Amber, toasty, malty |
| Dunkel | 1.048 - 1.056 | 4.5 - 5.6% | 18 - 28 | Dark, bready, Munich malt |
| Bock / Doppelbock | 1.064 - 1.112 | 6.3 - 10% | 16 - 35 | Strong, malty lagers |
| Weissbier | 1.044 - 1.052 | 4.3 - 5.6% | 8 - 15 | Wheat, banana, clove |
| Kölsch | 1.044 - 1.050 | 4.4 - 5.2% | 18 - 30 | Cool-fermented ale, delicate |
| Altbier | 1.044 - 1.054 | 4.3 - 5.5% | 25 - 50 | Copper, clean, bitter |

## Key Ingredients
- **Pilsner and Munich malt**, with Vienna and wheat.
- **Noble hops**: Hallertau, Tettnang, Spalt, Saaz.
- **Lager yeast** (cold-fermented) or ale yeast for Kölsch, Alt and Weissbier.

## Technique
- Step or [decoction](/beer/techniques/mashing/decoction) mashing, or [Hochkurz](/beer/techniques/mashing/hochkurz).
- Lager at 8 - 12 C, then lager (store cold) for 4 - 8 weeks.
- Control temperature for Weissbier yeast to balance banana and clove.
