# Kegging
Kegging replaces dozens of bottles with a single pressurised container. It is faster, lets you serve on tap and reduces oxidation when done properly.

## Equipment
- Corny (ball lock or pin lock) keg, 19 L (5 gal)
- CO2 cylinder and regulator
- Gas and beer lines, disconnects
- Tap or picnic tap
- Fridge or kegerator
- Sanitiser and keg seals (spare o-rings)

## Process
1. Clean and sanitise the keg and fittings.
2. Purge with CO2 (fill with sanitiser and push out with CO2 for a near-zero oxygen environment).
3. Transfer beer into the keg, ideally closed (keg-to-keg or fermenter-to-keg under CO2).
4. Seal, purge headspace by pulsing CO2.
5. Carbonate.
6. Serve at 8 - 12 psi, with correctly sized beer line.

## Carbonation Methods

| Method | How | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Set and forget | 10 - 12 psi at 3 - 4 C (38 - 39 F) | 1 - 2 weeks |
| Force carbonate | 30 psi for 24 - 36 h, then reduce to serving pressure | 2 days |
| Priming sugar | Same as bottles, in the keg | 1 - 2 weeks |

## Guideline Pressure
At 3 C (38 F), approximately 12 psi gives 2.4 volumes of CO2. Use a carbonation chart for your temperature.

## Common Problems
- **Foamy pours.** Line too short, serving pressure too high, or beer too warm.
- **Flat beer.** Leak or too low pressure.
- **Off flavours.** Rubber seals and old lines. Replace regularly.
