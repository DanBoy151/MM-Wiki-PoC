# Bottle Conditioning
A small amount of sugar is added to beer at bottling. Remaining yeast ferments it in the sealed bottle, producing CO2 that dissolves into the beer.

## Priming Sugar Amounts
Approximate amount for 19 L (5 gal) of beer at about 20 C (68 F):

| Target CO2 | Corn sugar (dextrose) | Table sugar | DME |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 volumes | 100 g | 90 g | 140 g |
| 2.4 volumes | 130 g | 115 g | 180 g |
| 3.0 volumes | 175 g | 160 g | 240 g |

Use a priming calculator as beer temperature affects residual CO2.

## Method
1. Confirm FG is stable for at least two days.
2. Boil sugar in 250 - 500 mL water for 5 minutes, then cool.
3. Add to a sanitised bottling bucket.
4. Gently siphon beer into the bucket to mix without splashing.
5. Fill bottles, leaving about 2.5 cm (1 inch) headspace.
6. Cap immediately.
7. Condition at 20 - 22 C (68 - 72 F) for 2 weeks, then refrigerate.

## Bottle Safety
- Use bottles designed for carbonated drinks. Not twist-off screw tops.
- Strong Belgian-style bottles for high carbonation.
- Store in a box, in case of a bottle bomb.
- Never prime beer that is not fully fermented.

## Tips
- **Pour carefully** so yeast sediment stays in the bottle.
- **Chill for 48 hours** before serving to help CO2 dissolve.
- **Be patient.** Beers over 6% ABV can take 4 weeks or longer to carbonate.
