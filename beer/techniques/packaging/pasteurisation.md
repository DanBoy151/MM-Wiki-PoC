# Pasteurisation
Heating packaged beer kills yeast and spoilage microbes, which stops fermentation and extends shelf life. Used for sweet beers, fruit beers, ciders and beers that contain residual sugar.

## Pasteurisation Units
One PU equals one minute at 60 C (140 F). Beer typically needs **15 - 25 PU** for stability. Higher temperatures count for more:

| Temperature | PU per minute (approx) |
|---|---|
| 55 C | 0.18 |
| 60 C | 1.0 |
| 65 C | 5.6 |
| 70 C | 31 |

## Home Bath Method (bottles or cans)
1. Carbonate and seal in sound, pressure-rated bottles or cans.
2. Place in a pot of water with a rack under them, then heat gently so the beer reaches 60 C (140 F).
3. Hold at 60 C for 15 - 20 minutes (check with a probe in a sacrificial container).
4. Remove carefully and cool gradually. Do not shock with cold water.

## Safety
- **Pressure rises** with temperature. Bottles may burst. Use a lid and protect yourself.
- **Heating too fast** can break glass.
- **Don't pasteurise unfinished fermentation** - the pressure is dangerous.

## Side Effects
- Slight dulling of hop aroma and a cooked or bready flavour.
- Not suitable for most hoppy beers.

## Alternatives
- Sterile filtering plus kegging.
- Chemical stabilisation (potassium sorbate and metabisulphite), mainly for cider and mead.
