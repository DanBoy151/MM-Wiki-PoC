# Mashing
Mashing is soaking crushed malt in hot water so that enzymes convert starch into fermentable and unfermentable sugars.

## Enzymes and Temperature

| Enzyme | Optimum | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Beta-amylase | 60 - 65 C (140 - 149 F) | Highly fermentable maltose. Dry, thin beer |
| Alpha-amylase | 67 - 72 C (153 - 162 F) | More dextrins. Fuller, sweeter beer |
| Protease | 45 - 55 C (113 - 131 F) | Breaks proteins. Rarely needed with modern malt |

A mash at **65 - 67 C (149 - 153 F)** suits most beers.

## Mash Methods

| Method | Description | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| [Partial Mash](/beer/techniques/mashing/partial-mash) | Small mash combined with malt extract | Low |
| [Single Infusion](/beer/techniques/mashing/single-infusion) | One rest at a single temperature | Low |
| [Hochkurz](/beer/techniques/mashing/hochkurz) | German two-rest short mash | Medium |
| [Kesselmaischen](/beer/techniques/mashing/kesselmaischen) | Step mash heated directly in the kettle | Medium |
| [Decoction](/beer/techniques/mashing/decoction) | Portions of mash boiled and returned | High |

## Ratios and Targets
- **Water to grain:** 2.5 - 3.5 L per kg (1.25 - 1.75 qt per lb).
- **Duration:** 60 minutes typical. Check conversion with an iodine test.
- **Mash pH:** 5.2 - 5.6. See [Water](/beer/ingredients/water).
- **Mash out:** raising to 76 C (170 F) stops enzymes and makes the wort more fluid for lautering.

## Lautering and Sparging
After the mash, the sweet wort is separated from the grain (**lautering**) and the grain bed is rinsed with hot water (**sparging**) to collect remaining sugars. Sparge at about 76 C (170 F) - hotter extracts tannins.
