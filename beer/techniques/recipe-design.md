# Recipe Design
A recipe is a set of choices to hit targets: strength, bitterness, colour, and flavour.

## Step by Step
1. **Choose a [style](/beer/styles)** and read its guidelines.
2. **Set targets** for OG, FG, ABV, IBU and colour (SRM or EBC).
3. **Choose a [grist](/beer/ingredients/malt/grain):** typically 80 - 95% base malt, with specialty malts to taste.
4. **Calculate quantities** for the volume and your brewhouse efficiency.
5. **Choose [hops](/beer/ingredients/hops)** and a schedule to hit IBU and aroma goals.
6. **Choose [yeast](/beer/ingredients/yeast)** and fermentation temperature.
7. **Check [water](/beer/ingredients/water)** is appropriate.
8. **Brew, taste, adjust.**

## Useful Formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gravity points | `(OG - 1) x 1000`. 1.050 = 50 points |
| Points from malt | `Weight (lb) x ppg x efficiency / volume (gal)` |
| ABV | `(OG - FG) x 131.25` |
| Apparent attenuation | `(OG - FG) / (OG - 1) x 100` |
| BU:GU ratio | `IBU / gravity points`. Around 0.5 is balanced, below 0.4 malty, above 0.7 bitter |

## Typical Grist Percentages

| Type | Share of grist |
|---|---|
| Base malt | 80 - 100% |
| Crystal / caramel | 0 - 15% |
| Roasted malts | 0 - 10% (stouts and porters) |
| Adjuncts (oats, wheat, sugar) | 0 - 20% |

## Common Mistakes
- Too many specialty malts. Aim for 2 - 3 at most to start.
- Too much crystal malt gives cloying sweetness.
- Chasing high ABV without scaling [yeast pitch](/beer/techniques/fermentation/yeast-management).
- Not accounting for efficiency in your first all grain brews.

## Tools
Any brewing software will do the maths (BeerSmith, Brewfather, Brewer's Friend or similar free calculators).
