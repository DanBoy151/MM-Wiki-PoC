# Dry Hops
Dry hopping adds hops to cool beer, usually in the fermenter or keg. No boiling means essential oils stay intact, giving a big aroma without added bitterness.

## Guidelines

| Parameter | Guide |
|---|---|
| Rate | 2 - 5 g/L (0.25 - 0.65 oz/gal) for standard, up to 10+ g/L for hazy IPA |
| Temperature | 15 - 20 C (59 - 68 F) |
| Contact time | 2 - 5 days. Longer risks grassy flavours |
| Form | Pellets work faster and better than whole leaf |

## Timing Options
- **After fermentation.** Classic. Lowest risk of fermentation off flavours.
- **During active fermentation.** Yeast biotransformation changes hop compounds into fruity aromas. Popular for hazy IPAs.
- **In the keg.** Fresh, but takes care to avoid clogging.

## Cautions
- **Oxygen.** Hops introduce oxygen. Purge with CO2 where possible. Oxidised hoppy beer turns dull and dark.
- **Hop creep.** Enzymes in hops can restart fermentation, increasing gravity drop and carbonation.
- **Grassy flavours** from over-long contact time.
- **Absorbed beer.** Hops soak up about 8 - 10 times their weight, so expect some loss.
