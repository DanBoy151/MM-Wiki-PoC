# Non-Saccharomyces Fermentation
Most beer uses [Saccharomyces](/beer/ingredients/yeast/saccharomyces). Other organisms give acidity, unusual flavours, or low-alcohol beer.

## Organisms

| Organism | Use |
|---|---|
| [*Brettanomyces*](/beer/ingredients/yeast/brett) | Funk, dryness, tropical fruit |
| [*Lactobacillus*, *Pediococcus*](/beer/ingredients/yeast/bugs) | Sourness |
| *Lachancea thermotolerans* | Lactic acid from sugar, gives clean tartness |
| *Pichia kluyveri* | Fruity esters, low alcohol |
| *Saccharomycodes ludwigii* | Doesn't ferment maltose. Used for low and no alcohol beer |
| Spontaneous (coolship) | Mixed wild microbes in lambic-style brewing |

## Approaches
- **Single strain.** Some commercial pure cultures are sold for sour or low alcohol beers.
- **Sequential.** Ferment with non-Saccharomyces first, then add brewer's yeast.
- **Co-pitch.** Pitch together.

## Practical Notes
- Many non-Saccharomyces strains **ferment poorly** and produce less alcohol. Expect a lower attenuation.
- They may need **lower hopping** and a lower pH.
- **Contamination risk** to later batches. Use separate plastics.
- Test the final gravity for stability before packaging.
