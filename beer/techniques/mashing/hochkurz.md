# Hochkurz
*Hochkurzmaischverfahren* ("high-short mashing") is a German step mash designed for fast, efficient brewing with well-modified malt. It uses two main rests, one at a lower temperature for maltose, and a short one at a higher temperature for dextrins, so that the brewer controls fermentability.

## Typical Schedule

| Step | Temperature | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mash in | 57 - 58 C (135 - 136 F) | 10 - 15 min | Brief rest |
| Maltose rest | 62 - 64 C (144 - 147 F) | 30 - 45 min | Fermentable sugars via beta-amylase |
| Saccharification | 70 - 72 C (158 - 162 F) | 20 - 30 min | Dextrins and conversion via alpha-amylase |
| Mash out | 76 - 78 C (169 - 172 F) | 5 min | Stops enzymes |

Schedules vary by brewery. Treat these as starting points and adjust for your malt.

## Effect
- **Maltose rest length** controls dryness. Longer gives a drier, more attenuated beer.
- **Higher rest** controls body and mouthfeel.

## When to Use
- Crisp, dry German lagers such as Helles and Pilsner.
- When you want more control over fermentability than [single infusion](/beer/techniques/mashing/single-infusion), without the time of a [decoction](/beer/techniques/mashing/decoction).

## Equipment
Needs a way to heat the mash, either direct fire, an electric element or RIMS, or infusions of boiling water. See [Kesselmaischen](/beer/techniques/mashing/kesselmaischen).
