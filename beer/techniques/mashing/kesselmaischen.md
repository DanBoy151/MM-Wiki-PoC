# Kesselmaischen
*Kesselmaischen* is German for "kettle mashing". The mash is carried out in the brew kettle (or a single direct-heated vessel) with heat applied to raise it through the rests, rather than infusing hot water into a separate mash tun or pulling decoctions.

## How It Works
1. Mash in at the first rest temperature.
2. Apply heat while stirring to reach each subsequent rest.
3. Hold at each rest, then continue to mash out.
4. Transfer or lauter in the same vessel with a bag, or move to a lauter tun.

## Advantages
- One vessel keeps equipment simple. Same method as BIAB.
- Full control of step temperatures without volume changes from infusions.
- Suits step schedules such as [Hochkurz](/beer/techniques/mashing/hochkurz).

## Risks
- **Scorching.** The thick mash sits on the heat source. Stir constantly and heat gently, or use a recirculating system.
- **Temperature overshoot.** Moving between steps at about 1 C (2 F) per minute is a good target.
- **Husk damage.** Aggressive stirring crushes husks and extracts tannins.
