# Partial Mash
A partial mash makes some of the wort sugar by mashing grain on a small scale. The rest comes from [malt extract](/beer/ingredients/malt/malt-extract). It bridges extract and all grain brewing.

## Why Do It
- Adds fresh grain character that extract can't give.
- Needs only a small pot or a bag. No big mash tun is required.
- Lets you use base malts like Maris Otter, which you cannot steep.
- A stepping stone to all grain.

## Basic Method
1. Mash 1.5 - 3 kg (3 - 7 lb) of crushed grain with 1.25 - 1.5 qt per lb of water at 65 - 67 C (150 - 153 F) for 60 minutes. Hold the temperature in an oven or a towel-wrapped pot.
2. Drain the grain and rinse with 70 - 76 C (160 - 170 F) water.
3. Combine the runnings with water in the kettle and add malt extract off the heat.
4. Boil and hop as usual.

## Rules of Thumb
- Base malt in the mash must have enough enzymes. Pale, Pilsner and Munich malts are fine.
- Keep at least 1/3 of the total fermentables as mashed grain to get the full benefit.
- Mashed grain contributes approximately 25 - 30 ppg in small systems.
