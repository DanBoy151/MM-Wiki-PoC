# Single Infusion
Grain is mixed with water at a single temperature and held for 45 - 75 minutes. Because modern malt is well modified, single infusion works for almost every style and is the usual starting point for all grain brewers.

## Steps
1. **Calculate strike water temperature** (the water heated before adding grain).
2. **Mix grain and water** to reach the target mash temperature.
3. **Hold** for 60 minutes, insulated. Stir at 15 and 30 minutes.
4. **Mash out** (optional): raise to 76 C (170 F).
5. **Lauter and sparge.**

## Strike Water Calculation
US units, with R = water to grain ratio (qt/lb):

`Strike temp (F) = (0.2 / R) x (Target - Grain temp) + Target`

Example: 1.25 qt/lb, target 152 F, grain at 68 F: (0.2 / 1.25) x (152 - 68) + 152 = **165 F**.

Add 2 - 3 F for mash tun thermal loss, or pre-heat your tun.

## Choosing Temperature

| Target | Result | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 64 - 65 C (147 - 149 F) | Dry, highly fermentable | Saison, Pilsner, IPA |
| 66 - 67 C (151 - 153 F) | Balanced | Most beers |
| 68 - 70 C (154 - 158 F) | Full body, sweet | Stouts, sweet styles |

## When Single Infusion Is Not Enough
Highly under-modified malts, rye-heavy or unmalted grist, or traditional German styles may benefit from [Hochkurz](/beer/techniques/mashing/hochkurz) or [Decoction](/beer/techniques/mashing/decoction).
