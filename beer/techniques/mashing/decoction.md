# Decoction
A decoction mash raises the temperature of the main mash by removing a portion (about one third, mostly thick grain), boiling it and returning it. It is the traditional method for Czech and German lagers, developed before thermometers and well-modified malt.

## Types

| Type | Boils | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Single decoction | 1 | Bocks, Dunkel, Märzen |
| Double decoction | 2 | Czech Pilsner, Bohemian lagers |
| Triple decoction | 3 | Traditional Pilsner, historic methods |

## Example Double Decoction
| Step | Temp | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Mash in | 37 - 40 C (99 - 104 F) | Acid rest, 10 - 20 minutes |
| Pull 1 | | Remove 1/3 thick mash, heat to 70 C, rest 15 min, boil 15 - 20 min |
| Return 1 | 52 - 55 C | Raises main mash to protein rest |
| Pull 2 | | Another 1/3 portion, same process |
| Return 2 | 65 - 67 C | Saccharification rest, 30 - 60 min |
| Mash out | 76 C (170 F) | Pull a thin portion or direct heat |

## Why Bother?
- **Melanoidin flavour.** Boiling the mash browns it and adds a rich, bready malt character.
- **Better extraction.** Gelatinises starch in under-modified malt.
- **Tradition and style authenticity.**

## Drawbacks
- Time (4 - 6 hours).
- Risk of scorching and tannin extraction. Stir constantly and keep the husks from being crushed.
- Modern malts reduce the need. Many brewers say the difference is subtle.

## Tips
- Pull mostly thick mash (grains), not liquid.
- Calculate volume to hit the target step. Software handles this well.
- Use a heat source that is not too fierce.
