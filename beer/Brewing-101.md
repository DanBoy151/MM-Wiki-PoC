# Brewing 101
A beginner's guide to brewing your first beer at home. Every method below makes a 5 gallon (19 L) batch of a simple American Pale Ale, so you can compare the three approaches side by side.

> New to brewing? Start with **Extract**. You can move up to Partial Mash and All Grain later and reuse most of the same equipment.
{.is-info}

## Choose Your Method

| | [Extract](#extract-brewing) | [Partial Mash](#partial-mash-brewing) | [All Grain](#all-grain-brewing) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Difficulty** | Easiest | Intermediate | Advanced |
| **Brew day length** | 3 - 4 hours | 4 - 5 hours | 5 - 7 hours |
| **Sugar source** | Malt extract | Extract + small mash | Fully mashed grain |
| **Extra equipment** | None | Mash bag, small pot | Large kettle, mash tun, sparge setup |
| **Control over recipe** | Limited | Good | Complete |
| **Cost per batch** | Highest | Medium | Lowest |

## Before You Start

### The Four Ingredients
Every beer is made from the same four things:

| Ingredient | Job |
|---|---|
| **Water** | Makes up about 90% of beer. If your tap water tastes good, it will generally brew good beer. Use a carbon filter or a Campden tablet if it is heavily chlorinated. |
| **Malt** | Provides the fermentable sugar, colour and body. |
| **Hops** | Add bitterness to balance sweetness, plus flavour and aroma. |
| **Yeast** | Eats the sugar and produces alcohol and CO2. |

### The Golden Rule: Sanitation
Anything that touches the beer **after the boil** must be cleaned and sanitised. Most failed first batches are infections, not bad recipes.

- **Clean** removes visible dirt (use an unscented oxygen cleaner such as PBW or OxiClean Free).
- **Sanitise** kills microbes (use Star San or Iodophor, following the packet dilution).
- Do not rinse Star San off; the foam is harmless.
- Never use bleach, scented soaps or scratched plastic that cannot be cleaned properly.

### Brew Day Essentials
These are needed for **every** method:

- Fermenter with lid and airlock (6.5 gal / 25 L bucket or carboy)
- Sanitiser and cleaner
- Hydrometer and test jar (to measure gravity)
- Thermometer
- Long spoon (stainless or food-grade plastic)
- Auto-siphon and tubing
- Bottles, caps and capper (about 48 x 12 oz or 53 x 355 ml), or a keg setup
- Bottling bucket with spigot and bottling wand
- Notebook to record every brew

> Keep a brew log. Write down ingredients, temperatures, times and gravity readings. It is the fastest way to improve.

## Extract Brewing
Malt extract is concentrated wort that someone else has already mashed and boiled down. You dissolve it in water, boil it with hops and ferment. It is the simplest route to good beer.

### Equipment
In addition to the [brew day essentials](#brew-day-essentials):

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Brew kettle, 4 gal / 15 L or larger | Stainless steel is best. Aluminium works. Avoid enamel with chips. |
| Stove or burner | A kitchen stove is fine for a 3 gal boil. |
| Steeping bag (muslin or nylon) | For specialty grains. |
| Strainer or funnel | Optional, for the transfer. |
| Ice or immersion chiller | To cool the wort quickly. An ice bath in a sink works for a first batch. |

### Ingredients
**Pale Ale, 5 gal / 19 L. Target OG ~1.048, ABV ~4.8%, IBU ~35**

| Ingredient | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pale liquid malt extract (LME) | 6 lb / 2.7 kg | Fermentable sugar |
| Crystal 40L malt (steeping grain) | 1 lb / 450 g | Colour, caramel flavour, body |
| Cascade hops (~6% AA) | 1 oz / 28 g at 60 min | Bittering |
| Cascade hops | 1 oz / 28 g at 5 min | Flavour and aroma |
| Safale US-05 dry yeast | 1 packet (11.5 g) | Clean American ale yeast |
| Corn sugar (dextrose) | 5 oz / 140 g | Priming for bottling |

### Process
1. **Sanitise** your fermenter, airlock and anything else that will touch cooled wort.
2. **Steep the grains.** Heat 3 gal / 11 L of water to 160 F / 71 C. Put the crushed Crystal malt in the steeping bag and steep for 20 minutes. Do not let it go above 170 F / 77 C, which extracts harsh tannins.
3. **Remove the bag** and let it drain. Do not squeeze it.
4. **Bring to a boil**, then turn off the heat and **stir in the LME** until fully dissolved. Adding it off the heat prevents scorching on the bottom of the kettle.
5. **Return to a boil** and add the 60 minute hops. Start a 60 minute timer.
6. **Watch for boil-overs** at the start. A spray bottle of water helps.
7. **At 5 minutes remaining**, add the flavour/aroma hops.
8. **Cool the wort** to below 75 F / 24 C as quickly as you can. Fast cooling gives clearer beer and reduces infection risk.
9. **Transfer to the fermenter** and top up to 5 gal / 19 L with cold, clean water.
10. **Take a gravity reading** (original gravity, OG) with the hydrometer and write it down.
11. **Pitch the yeast**, seal with the lid and airlock, and move on to [Fermentation and Bottling](#fermentation-and-bottling-all-methods).

## Partial Mash Brewing
Partial mash replaces part of the extract with sugar you extract from grain yourself. You get more fresh-grain flavour and a bit more control, without needing the large equipment of all grain.

### Equipment
Everything from [Extract Brewing](#equipment), plus:

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Large mesh grain bag (BIAB style) | Holds the base malt during the mash. |
| Second pot, 2 - 3 gal / 8 - 12 L | Used for mashing and heating sparge water. |
| Colander or large strainer | Sits over the kettle for draining the bag. |
| Oven (optional) | Holds mash temperature steady. Set to the lowest setting, around 150 F / 65 C. |
| pH strips (optional) | Helpful but not required. |

### Ingredients
**Pale Ale, 5 gal / 19 L. Target OG ~1.047, ABV ~4.7%, IBU ~35**

| Ingredient | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pale liquid malt extract (LME) | 4 lb / 1.8 kg | Fermentable sugar |
| Pale 2-row malt (crushed) | 3 lb / 1.4 kg | Mashed for sugar and fresh malt flavour |
| Crystal 40L malt (crushed) | 8 oz / 225 g | Mashed with the pale malt |
| Cascade hops (~6% AA) | 1 oz / 28 g at 60 min | Bittering |
| Cascade hops | 1 oz / 28 g at 5 min | Flavour and aroma |
| Safale US-05 dry yeast | 1 packet (11.5 g) | Clean American ale yeast |
| Corn sugar (dextrose) | 5 oz / 140 g | Priming for bottling |

> A **mash** soaks crushed malt in hot water. Enzymes in the malt convert starch into fermentable sugar. Temperature controls the result: lower (148 - 150 F / 64 - 66 C) gives a drier beer, higher (154 - 156 F / 68 - 69 C) gives a fuller, sweeter beer.
{.is-info}

### Process
1. **Sanitise** your fermenter and equipment.
2. **Heat mash water.** In the second pot, heat 1.5 gal / 5.7 L (about 1.5 qt per lb of grain) to 162 F / 72 C.
3. **Mash in.** Put the grain bag in the pot, pour in the crushed pale and Crystal malts and stir well to remove dry clumps. Target a mash temperature of **152 F / 67 C**.
4. **Hold the mash for 60 minutes**, covered. Wrap the pot in a towel or put it in the oven to hold temperature. Check at 15 minutes and stir if needed.
5. **Heat sparge water.** Meanwhile heat 2 gal / 7.5 L of water to 170 F / 77 C in the kettle.
6. **Sparge.** Lift the bag, let it drain over the kettle and slowly pour the sparge water over the grain. Collect the liquid (wort) in the kettle. Do not squeeze the bag hard.
7. **Bring to a boil** with about 3 gal / 11 L of wort. Turn off the heat and **stir in the LME** until dissolved.
8. **Return to a boil**, add the 60 minute hops and start a 60 minute timer.
9. **At 5 minutes remaining**, add the flavour/aroma hops.
10. **Cool, transfer and top up** to 5 gal / 19 L, take the original gravity reading and **pitch the yeast**, as in steps 8 - 11 of [Extract Brewing](#process).
11. Continue with [Fermentation and Bottling](#fermentation-and-bottling-all-methods).

## All Grain Brewing
All grain brewing makes the entire wort from malted grain. It gives you total control over recipe, is the cheapest per batch and is how commercial breweries work. The process is the same as partial mash on a larger scale.

### Equipment
In addition to the [brew day essentials](#brew-day-essentials):

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Large kettle, 8 - 10 gal / 30 - 38 L | Needs to hold the full pre-boil volume of about 6.5 gal / 25 L plus headroom. |
| Strong heat source | A propane burner or a large electric element. A kitchen stove may struggle. |
| Mash tun | An insulated cooler with a false bottom or braid and a valve, **or** a BIAB (brew in a bag) setup using a large voile bag in the kettle. |
| Hot liquor tank or second pot | For sparge water. Not needed for no-sparge BIAB. |
| Immersion or counterflow chiller | Strongly recommended for the larger volume. |
| Grain mill (optional) | Many shops will crush grain for you. |
| Digital thermometer | Mash temperature must be accurate. |

> **BIAB vs. Mash Tun.** Brew in a Bag is the cheapest way into all grain. Use a large bag in the kettle, mash in the kettle, then lift the bag out. Mash tun systems cost more but scale better and allow a proper sparge.
{.is-info}

### Ingredients
**Pale Ale, 5 gal / 19 L. Target OG ~1.051, ABV ~5.2%, IBU ~35** (assumes about 70% brewhouse efficiency)

| Ingredient | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pale 2-row malt (crushed) | 9 lb / 4.1 kg | Base malt, provides most of the sugar |
| Crystal 40L malt (crushed) | 1 lb / 450 g | Colour, caramel flavour, body |
| Cascade hops (~6% AA) | 1 oz / 28 g at 60 min | Bittering |
| Cascade hops | 1 oz / 28 g at 5 min | Flavour and aroma |
| Safale US-05 dry yeast | 1 packet (11.5 g) | Clean American ale yeast |
| Corn sugar (dextrose) | 5 oz / 140 g | Priming for bottling |

### Process
1. **Sanitise** your fermenter and everything that touches cooled wort.
2. **Heat the strike water.** Use about 1.25 qt per lb of grain: 3 gal / 11.5 L heated to roughly 163 F / 73 C. The water should be hotter than your target because adding grain cools it.
3. **Mash in.** Add the crushed grain to the water slowly, stirring to break up dough balls. Target **152 F / 67 C**. Adjust with small additions of hot or cold water.
4. **Mash for 60 minutes**, covered and insulated. Check the temperature at 15 and 30 minutes.
5. **Heat sparge water.** Heat 4 gal / 15 L to 170 F / 77 C while the mash rests.
6. **Vorlauf.** Slowly drain the first litre or two and pour it gently back on top of the grain bed until the runnings are clear.
7. **Sparge.** Slowly run the wort into the kettle while adding sparge water on top at the same rate. Take 30 - 45 minutes. Stop at about 6.5 gal / 25 L pre-boil volume. Stop earlier if the runnings taste harsh or are below 1.010 gravity.
8. **Boil for 60 minutes.** Add the 60 minute hops when the wort reaches a rolling boil and start the timer. Expect to lose about 1 gal / 3.8 L to evaporation.
9. **At 5 minutes remaining**, add the flavour/aroma hops (and a whirlfloc tablet or Irish moss at 15 minutes if you have one, for clearer beer).
10. **Cool** to below 75 F / 24 C as quickly as possible.
11. **Transfer** to the fermenter, leaving the trub (hop and protein sludge) behind where you can.
12. **Take the original gravity reading**, record it, then **pitch the yeast**.
13. Continue with [Fermentation and Bottling](#fermentation-and-bottling-all-methods).

## Fermentation and Bottling (all methods)
Once the wort is in the fermenter and the yeast is pitched, all three methods are identical.

### Fermentation
1. **Seal** the fermenter and fit a sanitised airlock half-filled with sanitiser or vodka.
2. **Keep it at 64 - 68 F / 18 - 20 C** in a dark spot. Temperature matters: warm fermentation creates fruity and solvent-like flavours.
3. **Expect activity** within 12 - 24 hours (bubbling, krausen foam on top). It is normal for this to take up to 48 hours.
4. **Leave it alone** for 10 - 14 days. Resist the urge to open the lid.
5. **Check it is finished.** Take a hydrometer reading on two days at least 48 hours apart. If the reading is stable, usually ~1.010 - 1.012, fermentation is done. Bubbles in the airlock are not a reliable indicator.

### Bottling
1. **Calculate ABV:** `(OG - FG) x 131.25`. For example (1.048 - 1.011) x 131.25 = 4.9%.
2. **Sanitise** bottles, caps, bottling bucket, tubing and wand.
3. **Make priming solution.** Boil 5 oz / 140 g corn sugar in 2 cups / 500 ml of water for 5 minutes and cool to room temperature.
4. **Add the solution to the bottling bucket**, then siphon the beer on top gently so it mixes without splashing (splashing adds oxygen).
5. **Fill the bottles** leaving about 1 inch / 2.5 cm of headspace and cap immediately.
6. **Condition** the bottles at 68 - 72 F / 20 - 22 C for **2 weeks** to carbonate.
7. **Chill and enjoy.** Refrigerate for at least a few days before opening. Pour carefully to leave the yeast sediment in the bottle.

> **Patience pays.** Most beers taste significantly better at 4 - 6 weeks after bottling than at 2.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No fermentation after 48 hrs | Cold room, old or dead yeast, or leaking lid | Move somewhere warmer, check the hydrometer, re-pitch fresh yeast if gravity has not moved. |
| Lid or airlock blew off | Very vigorous fermentation | Fit a sanitised blow-off tube into a bucket of sanitiser. |
| Sweet, syrupy finished beer | Stuck or underpitched fermentation, too high mash temp | Warm gently, rouse the yeast, or pitch fresh yeast. |
| Medicinal or plastic flavour | Chlorine in water | Use filtered water or Campden tablets. |
| Buttery flavour | Short fermentation (diacetyl) | Leave on the yeast longer. |
| Green apple flavour | Beer bottled too early (acetaldehyde) | Wait for fermentation to finish and clean up. |
| Flat bottles | Too cold, too little sugar or leaky caps | Move somewhere warmer for another week. |
| Gushing or over-carbonated bottles | Bottled before fermentation was done, or infection | Chill thoroughly and open carefully over a sink. |
| White floating film or fuzzy patches | Infection | Discard the batch and review sanitation. |

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ABV** | Alcohol by volume. |
| **AA** | Alpha acid percentage of a hop, which determines its bittering power. |
| **Airlock** | One-way valve letting CO2 out without letting air in. |
| **BIAB** | Brew in a bag. All grain mashed in a bag inside the kettle. |
| **Boil-over** | Foaming wort spilling out of the kettle. |
| **FG** | Final gravity. Hydrometer reading when fermentation is complete. |
| **IBU** | International Bitterness Units. |
| **Krausen** | Foamy head that forms on top of fermenting beer. |
| **LME / DME** | Liquid / dry malt extract. |
| **Mash** | Soaking crushed malt in hot water to convert starch to sugar. |
| **OG** | Original gravity. Hydrometer reading before fermentation. |
| **Pitch** | To add yeast to the wort. |
| **Priming sugar** | Sugar added at bottling for carbonation. |
| **Sparge** | Rinsing the grain with hot water to collect remaining sugars. |
| **Trub** | Sediment of hops and proteins left after the boil. |
| **Wort** | Unfermented beer. |
